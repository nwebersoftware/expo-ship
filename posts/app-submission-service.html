I'm taking app submissions as fixed-fee work

Four of my apps are live on the App Store and Google Play: an idle game, a card game, a memory game, a neon puzzle. None of them passed review on the first submission, and only one failed for a reason I would have guessed in advance.

That gap between "it builds on my machine" and "there is a public link you can send people" is where most apps stall. The app is usually fine. The release is the problem. Signing, an icon with an alpha channel, a privacy manifest that has to declare reason codes for APIs you did not know you were calling, five screenshot sizes, a data-collection questionnaire that rejects your build because of one checkbox.

So it is a service now

Fixed fee per app, from $395. I take a working repo, build it, fix what blocks submission, do the store assets and the listing copy, submit to both stores, and handle the rejection if one comes back. Prices and scope are at booleanbros.com/ship. There is also a Fiverr listing if you would rather keep the money in escrow until it is done.

Two things I will not do. Promise approval: Apple can reject anything, and the fee buys the work, not the verdict. And take your developer accounts: you invite mine as an App Manager and a Play Console release user, the listing stays in your name, and you can revoke access the afternoon the app goes live.

What actually goes wrong

  • 4.2.2, minimum functionality. The app is a web view with extra steps. This one is not fixable with an argument, so the build needs real native capability: offline storage, notifications, share sheets, haptics.
  • 5.1.1(v), account deletion. If the app creates accounts, it has to let people delete them, server-side, without emailing support.
  • ITMS-91053. A required-reason API your build calls that PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy does not declare. Machine-generated, no human involved, easy to fix once you know which API it is.
  • ITMS-90717. Transparency in the 1024px icon. The upload fails with a code instead of a sentence.
  • Play Console data safety. The form asks about data your dependencies collect. Answer it wrong and the listing gets pulled weeks later, not at upload.

Who this is for

Mostly solo developers and small teams on Expo, React Native or Capacitor who got stuck at review, or never submitted because the checklist is longer than the app. The work starts at the repo and ends at a live listing, so it does not fit an app that is not built yet. That is development, and it is a different job.

If you are mid-rejection right now, send the guideline number and the review text. I can usually tell you in one message whether it is a build fix, a product fix, or a lost cause worth appealing.
